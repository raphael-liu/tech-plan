# Input understanding and multi-turn interviews

[简体中文](intake.md)

## Document-driven work

Identify the teams, periods, and purpose covered by each source. Locate goals, progress, risks, resources, decisions, and retrospectives before reading enough surrounding context to understand relationships. Use OCR for scans and inspect original pages when numbers, table associations, or important judgments matter. Report unreadable content rather than guessing.

Maintain a compact evidence ledger, attaching it to the plan when useful:

| ID | Date and source locator | Fact/statement and scope | Goal/problem/project | Type and confidence boundaries | Conflict or validation need |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Reconstruct the change chain: intended change → original rationale → investment and outputs → observed outcomes → changed constraints/judgments → continue, adapt, or stop. Keep planned, reported complete, accepted, and value verified as distinct states.

For conflicts, compare periods, measurement definitions, scope, and versions. Preserve both sources. Verify independently where possible, then ask focused questions if the conflict still affects decisions. Do not vote by document count or assume the latest statement proves an outcome.

## Conversation-driven work

Usually ask 1–3 related questions per turn that could affect the next decision. Allow natural narrative. Briefly state current understanding and its decision implications before asking. Do not repeat answered questions in new wording or open with dozens of questions covering all six sections.

First resolve direction-setting gaps: mission, intended outcomes, current gaps, hard constraints, and key conflicts. Then investigate the weakest link. These are optional prompts, not a required questionnaire:

| Area | High-information question | Decision affected |
|---|---|---|
| Value and boundaries | Whose outcome must change by the end of this period? What can this team not decide? | Goals and authority |
| Diagnosis | What events and data support this? What distinguishes technical bottlenecks from incentives or process issues? | Causes and validation |
| Trade-offs | What would you defer to protect this? Who bears that cost? Would more resources change your choice? | Opportunity cost and constraints |
| Prioritization | Which output must exist first? Which initiatives need the same scarce specialist? What survives if only one can proceed? | Dependencies and feasible effort |
| Alignment | Who committed which resources, outputs, and dates? What evidence records it? Who might object? | Commitment and resistance |
| Execution cadence | Where can we first test the judgment and still stop? What prevents expansion? How would we recover? | Risk and fallback |
| Recalibration | What would make you abandon the judgment? When could we observe it? Which decision would we revisit? | Revised judgment and stop-loss |

When a user proposes a solution, understand its intended outcome, evidence, and constraints before treating it as a requirement or a mistake. For disagreements, identify competing explanations and the minimum evidence needed to distinguish them.

## Continuity and stopping conditions

Retain a compact record of facts and sources, diagnosis, decisions/assumptions, excluded paths, unresolved conflicts, next questions, and affected sections. Show it only when useful for checking understanding, not in full every turn.

Draft once the goal and horizon are clear, main constraints identified, diagnosis supported or testable, choices and priorities explainable, and resources/commitments distinguished from prerequisites. Unknown owners, thresholds, or budgets can remain explicit limitations in a draft.

If missing information could reverse direction or make a schedule uncommittable, resolve it before relying on it while continuing independent work. If the user declines further questions, deliver conditional options and minimal open decisions. Silence is not agreement.

## Cross-language evidence

Use the requested interview and output languages independently of source language. Preserve the original source title and locator; explanatory translations can follow. Reconcile aliases across languages without merging distinct systems or teams. Keep estimates, proposals, commitments, acceptance, and verified benefits distinct in translation. On a language switch, carry forward the evidence ledger and open questions instead of interviewing again.
